# Go-To-Market (GTM) for Digital Products

> **This skill exists to stop:** "the product is done — marketing, go get users," followed by ad spend into a funnel that loses half its users at onboarding.


## 🤖 0. HOW TO USE (agent workflow)
**A. Sequence check first (the million-dollar rule):** Retention → Conversion → Acquisition. If retention of a small organic cohort is unknown, refuse to plan paid acquisition; plan the retention measurement instead.
**B. Four work zones:** 5C analysis → STP + one positioning statement co-signed by Product, Growth, and Sales → marketing mix in SAVE terms → acquisition funnel drawn on the real journey (not a generic AARRR).
**C. Pick the school:** waterfall master plan (big budget, existing channels) vs growth-loop iteration (startup/SaaS: launch small, experiment, learn, iterate).
**D. Pick the first customer:** early adopters with an acute pain, never the mass market on day one.
**Standard output:** positioning statement · SAVE table · journey funnel with a metric per step · the retention gate that must pass before spend · first-segment definition.

## 1. The sequence rule — Retention → Conversion → Acquisition
Real case from the workshop: a new product lost 50% of users at the first onboarding step. Had the team run ads, every 100 users bought would have yielded 50 who left without understanding the product. Correct order: measure retention on a small organic cohort → fix the onboarding drop (50% → 20%) with UX and engineering → only then scale acquisition, and only while CAC < LTV.

## 2. Four work zones
1. **5C:** Company (real resources) · Customers (pain, behavior) · Competitors (direct and indirect) · **Collaborators** (distribution partners you depend on — app stores, marketplaces, resellers) · Climate (regulation, culture).
2. **STP & positioning:** Product, Growth, and Sales sit in one room and sign one statement: *"For [segment] who struggle with [pain], [product] delivers [solution], better than [alternative] because [core USP]."*
3. **SAVE, not 4P:** Solution (sell the pain solved, not the feature list) · Access (always-on digital access, not a shelf) · Value (priced on ROI the customer feels) · **Education** (for products nobody has used before, teaching the market — docs, webinars, case studies, walkthroughs — IS the core GTM activity, not promotion).
4. **Acquisition funnel on the real journey:** Impression → Click → Landing/Install → First activation → Retention → Paid — each step with its metric; don't paste a textbook AARRR.

## 3. Two schools
| Waterfall plan | Growth-loop iteration |
|---|---|
| Master plan → multi-channel launch → campaign review | Launch MVP → short experiments → measure → insight → iterate |
| Big corp, FMCG, banking, big budgets, existing channels | Startups, SaaS, digital products, constrained resources |

## 4. Whom to sell to first
Innovators (~2.5%) → **Early adopters (~13.5%) — start here**: acute pain, tolerate rough edges, cheapest CAC → the chasm → early majority (needs social proof) → late majority. Never launch to the mass market first: it demands polish, huge reach, and repetition.

## 5. Two debates worth remembering
- **B2B: buyer ≠ end user.** The CFO who signs is not the person who clicks. Cash flow that keeps the company alive can outrank end-user delight in the short run; then rebalance. Before a B2B launch, red-team the plan with your best salespeople and the CFO.
- **Marketplace chicken-and-egg:** the classic move was to fake supply by hand (buy from a retailer, ship it yourself) to measure *buyer retention* before recruiting sellers at scale. Prove the loop closes before scaling either side.

## Applied context (edtech)
Retention gate: do guests who take one free graded test return within 7 days? Measure on organic traffic before any paid push. Positioning statement co-signed by product, growth, and admissions. SAVE: Education is the core motion — learners have never seen AI grading calibrated on real exam data, so walkthroughs and sample reports outrank discounts. First segment: learners stuck at the same band for two attempts (acute pain), not "everyone preparing for the exam".
